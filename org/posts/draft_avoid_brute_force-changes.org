Maintaining your emacs configuration teaches you something about actual software
maintenance.  Perhaps the toughest lesson for me is to stop myself form trying
to brute force changes.  What do I mean?  Well when you have a large emacs
configuration it happens that you want to replace some code that at first you
thought was great and beautiful but now you think is horrible.  The temptation
is to just quickly "bruteforce" it.  But when you have code that works and that
took a while to write, typically you can't just remove it and replace it easily.
Instead you need to make a plan wherein you will step by step, little by little,
slowly chip at it, replace parts of it until it is all completely replaced.
When you have a small and simple emacs config this does not apply.
